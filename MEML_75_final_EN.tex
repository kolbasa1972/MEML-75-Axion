\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We present model MEML~75, which parametrizes the quark sector of the
Standard Model through the geometry of the six-dimensional
orbifold \(T^2/\mathbb{Z}_3\) with two independent tori.
The model has \textbf{11 nominal parameters} (including the ALP
misalignment angle \(\theta_i\)), of which one is fixed by an
unphysical overall scale (\(a_u = 1\)): \textbf{10 effective parameters}
fit \textbf{9 quark observables}, and adding the ALP misalignment angle
\(\theta_i\) and the relic density \(\Omega_a h^2\) gives
\textbf{11 effective parameters fitting 10 observables}
(\(N_{\rm dof} = -1\)). The model is therefore borderline overfitted,
which we state explicitly.

\textbf{The main testable consequence:}
axion-like dark matter with
\(f_a = 1.9801\times10^{11}\)~GeV,
\(m_a = 28.407\)~\(\mu\)eV, \(\nu_a = 6.8688\)~GHz, and ALP-photon
coupling
\(g_{a\gamma\gamma} = +4.38\times10^{-15}\)~GeV\(^{-1}\) (DFSZ)
or \(-1.13\times10^{-14}\)~GeV\(^{-1}\) (KSVZ), testable
at ADMX/HAYSTAC (0.5--10~GHz).
The resulting ALP mass also implies a black hole superradiance window at
\(M_{\rm BH} \sim 10^{25}\)~kg.
These are consequences of the chain \(\alpha_G \to \varepsilon \to f_a\),
not predictions from the orbifold geometry.

\textbf{Quark sector.} A global fit with loss function
\(\chi^2 = \sum_i ((O_i-O_i^{\rm target})/\sigma_i)^2\) yields
\(\chi^2 < 10^{-15}\) (numerical precision limit) at
\(\tau_u = -0.1577944085 + 2.3851782590\,i\),
\(\tau_d = +0.3693905617 + 1.5410496057\,i\).
The hierarchical Yukawa coefficients can be qualitatively understood
via the geometric overlap of wavefunctions at the orbifold fixed points,
with a universal scale \(\alpha \sim 10\) and appropriate prefactors.

\textbf{Lepton sector (negative result).} We attempted to extend the
modular construction to the leptons using \(3_S\) contractions of
\(Y^{(2)}\), \(Y^{(4)}\), \(Y^{(6)}\) and a uniform diagonal shift.
The \(3_S\) sector with \(Y^{(2)}\) and \(Y^{(4)}\) alone cannot
simultaneously reproduce the PMNS angles and the mass-squared
differences (best joint fit \(\chi^2 \approx 39\)). Adding
\(M_{3_S}(Y^{(6)})\) gives \(\chi^2 = 0\) formally, but the modulus
\(\tau_l\) becomes undetermined: six different values of
\(\tau_l\) (spread \(\Delta \mathrm{Re}\,\tau_l = 2.25\)) give equivalent
fits. A viable lepton sector requires a different modular structure;
we leave it for future work and do not include leptons in the main
results.

\textbf{Verdict:} the model is a phenomenological quark-sector + ALP
construction with a negative result on the lepton sector. It reproduces
the 9 quark observables with high precision and makes one independently
testable prediction (ALP dark matter at \(6.87\)~GHz).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Modular models of fermion masses~\cite{Feruglio2017,Kobayashi2019}
build the mass hierarchy and mixing from compactification geometry.
In model MEML~75, quarks are localized on two independent tori
\(T^2/\mathbb{Z}_3\) with moduli \(\tau_u\) and \(\tau_d\).
The mass matrices are built from \(A_4\) modular forms of weights
\((2,4,6)\). A third torus \(\tau_l\) for leptons was attempted but
does not yield a viable description; we report this negative result
in \S8.

\section{Geometry of \(T^2/\mathbb{Z}_3\)}
The orbifold \(T^2/\mathbb{Z}_3\) is defined by:
\begin{equation}
z = x + \tau y, \quad ds^2 = dx^2 + dx\,dy + dy^2,
\end{equation}
where \(\tau = e^{i\pi/3} = \frac12 + i\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\). The $Z_3$-action $z \to \omega z$ (with $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$) has three fixed points, providing a topological explanation for three generations.

\section{Bulk coupling \(\alpha_G\) (postulate)}
\begin{equation}
\alpha_G = \frac{2}{3\pi} = 0.2122065908.
\end{equation}
Geometric motivation: \(2/3\) is the volume fraction of the fundamental domain of the \(\mathbb{Z}_3\)-orbifold; \(1/\pi\) is the normalization of the theta-function period.

\paragraph{Geometric motivation via coarse-space topology.}
The numerical value \(2/(3\pi)\) can be read off from the topology of
the coarse space of \(T^2/\mathbb{Z}_3\). Under the \(\mathbb{Z}_3\) action,
the torus \(T^2\) collapses onto a sphere:
\(T^2/\mathbb{Z}_3 \cong S^2\), whose topological Euler characteristic is
\begin{equation}
\chi(S^2) = 2.
\end{equation}
The number of fixed points of the \(\mathbb{Z}_3\)-action is \(N_{\rm fix}=3\).
Combining these with the theta-function period normalization \(1/\pi\) gives
\begin{equation}
\alpha_G = \frac{\chi(S^2)}{N_{\rm fix}\,\pi}
= \frac{2}{3\pi}.
\end{equation}
We emphasize that this is a \emph{geometric motivation}, not a derivation:
the relation \(\alpha_G = \chi(S^2)/(N_{\rm fix}\pi)\) is not forced by
the orbifold geometry. The status of \(\alpha_G\) therefore remains that of
a postulate.

\section{Suppression factor \(\varepsilon\) (ansatz)}
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon = \left(\frac{\alpha_G}{4\pi}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{36\pi^4} = 2.851662\times10^{-4}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Two-loop interpretation.}
The factor \(4\pi\) in the denominator can be read as the standard two-loop
suppression \(1/(16\pi^2)\):
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon = \left(\frac{\alpha_G}{4\pi}\right)^2
= \frac{\alpha_G^2}{16\pi^2}.
\end{equation}
This provides a physical interpretation of the ansatz, but does not change its numerical value.
The status of \(\varepsilon\) therefore remains that of an ansatz.

\section{Axion-like (ALP) sector}
\subsection{ALP parameters}
\begin{align}
f_a &= M_{Pl}^{\rm red}\,\varepsilon^2 = 1.9801\times10^{11}~\text{GeV}, \\
m_a &= \frac{(\chi^{1/4})^2}{f_a} = 28.407~\mu\text{eV}, \\

\end{align}
Here \(\chi^{1/4} \approx 75.0\)~MeV is the fourth root of the topological susceptibility of QCD. We emphasize that no explicit \(U(1)_{PQ}\) symmetry is introduced; the object is an ALP.

The numerical values of \(f_a\) and \(m_a\) follow from the
chain \(\alpha_G \to \varepsilon \to f_a \to m_a\) (with \(\chi^{1/4}\) taken
from QCD), and not from a derivation of these quantities from the orbifold
geometry.

\subsection{ALP dark matter}
\begin{equation}
\Omega_a h^2 \approx 0.12\,\theta_i^2\, f_{\rm anh}(\theta_i)
\left(\frac{f_a}{3\times10^{11}}\right)^{7/6},
\end{equation}
where \(f_{\rm anh}(\theta_i) = [\ln(e/(1-(\theta_i/\pi)^2))]^{7/6}\) is the anharmonicity factor. Numerical $T$-effects introduce a \(+12.4\%\) correction, leading to agreement with Planck at \(\theta_i = 1.1162\).

\subsection{Black hole superradiance}
\label{sec:bh}
The ultra-light ALP mass obtained from the chain above has important astrophysical consequences. In the presence of a rotating black hole, the ALP field can extract rotational energy via the superradiance mechanism. The critical black hole mass for which this process is most efficient is given by:
\begin{equation}
M_{\rm BH} \simeq \frac{\hbar c}{G m_a}.
\end{equation}
For the resulting mass \(m_a = 28.407~\mu\)eV, this yields:
\begin{equation}
M_{\rm BH} \approx 9.36 \times 10^{24}~{\rm kg} \approx 4.7 \times 10^{-6}~M_\odot.
\end{equation}
The superradiance window is an algebraic consequence of the ALP mass \(m_a\); it does not constitute new physics beyond the ALP sector. We note that black holes in this mass range correspond to the primordial black hole (PBH) window. Currently, no direct detections of PBHs with \(M \sim 10^{-6} M_\odot\) have been confirmed by LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA. Microlensing surveys (such as OGLE) place strict upper bounds on the fraction of dark matter that can be composed of PBHs in this mass range, but these constraints do not exclude a subdominant population sufficient to test the superradiance effect.

\subsection{Results}
Independent optimization yields \(\chi^2 < 10^{-15}\) at the moduli given in the abstract. The residual \(\chi^2\) is the machine-precision limit of the numerical fit, not a physical measurement. The coefficients are:
\begin{equation}
c^{\rm fit} \approx (5.31\times10^{-5},\,7.19,\,0.039,\,2.43\times10^{-4},\,0.503,\,1.37\times10^{-3},\,2.22\times10^{-3}).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Calibration of the overall scale.}
The overall scale of each mass matrix is unphysical: the observables depend
only on mass ratios and CKM elements, which are invariant under
\(M_u \to k M_u\), \(M_d \to k M_d\). We fix \(a_u = 1\) without loss of generality.

\section{Attempted lepton extension (negative result)}
\label{sec:lepton_failed}
We attempted to extend the modular construction to the lepton sector
using the same \(3_S\) contractions of \(Y^{(2)}\) and \(Y^{(4)}\) that
appear in the quark sector, plus a uniform diagonal shift from the
singlet contractions
\(S_k = (Y_1^{(k)})^2 + 2 Y_2^{(k)} Y_3^{(k)}\):
% ИСПРАВЛЕНИЕ: Обернули в split, чтобы формула красиво переносилась
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
M_\nu &= g_1 M_{3_S}(Y^{(2)}) + g_2 M_{3_S}(Y^{(4)}) \\
&\quad + \left( g_3 S_2 + g_4 S_4 + g_5 S_6 \right) \mathbb{I}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
We fitted five lepton observables --- the three PMNS angles
\(\theta_{12},\theta_{13},\theta_{23}\) and the two mass-squared
differences \(\Delta m^2_{21},\Delta m^2_{31}\) (NuFIT 5.2, normal
ordering) --- and found two problems.

\paragraph{(i) Without $Y^{(6)}$: insufficient structure.}
With \(g_1,g_2\) and the diagonal shift, the best joint fit gives
\(\chi^2 \approx 39\), with the residual localized in
\(\theta_{23}\) (\(\theta_{23} \approx 46^\circ\) vs.\ target
\(49.1^\circ\)). The \(3_S\) structure with \(Y^{(2)}\) and \(Y^{(4)}\)
alone cannot simultaneously reproduce the three PMNS angles and the
two mass-squared differences.

\paragraph{(ii) With $Y^{(6)}$: loss of predictivity.}
Adding \(M_{3_S}(Y^{(6)})\) gives \(\chi^2 = 0\) formally, but the fit
succeeds for \emph{any} \(\tau_l\): six different values of \(\tau_l\)
(with spread \(\Delta \mathrm{Re}\,\tau_l = 2.25\) across the search
region) give equivalent fits with \(\chi^2 < 10^{-20}\). The modulus
\(\tau_l\) is therefore not determined by the lepton data, and the
modular structure of the lepton sector does not constrain the model.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
We do not include the lepton sector in the main results. A viable
lepton sector within the \(T^2/\mathbb{Z}_3\) modular framework
requires a different structure (e.g., a non-uniform diagonal shift,
additional modular forms, or a different \(S_3\) contraction).
We leave this for future work.

\paragraph{Parameter classes.}
The 11 parameters group into three classes:
(i) the four real geometric moduli of the two quark tori
(\(\tau_u\), \(\tau_d\)), which are the core of the model;
(ii) the six Lagrangian Yukawa coefficients
(\(b_u, c_u, a_1, a_2, b, c\)), which are phenomenological at this stage
--- deriving them from holomorphic 6D~$\to$~4D couplings is the
Critical item of the roadmap (\S12);
(iii) the ALP misalignment angle \(\theta_i\), which is a cosmological
initial condition rather than a Lagrangian parameter.

\paragraph{Information content.}
The model fits 9 quark observables with 10 effective parameters, and
10 observables (9 quarks + \(\Omega_a h^2\)) with 11 effective
parameters. In both cases \(N_{\rm dof} = -1\): the model is
borderline overfitted. This is common for string-motivated models,
but a reduction of parameters (e.g., deriving the Yukawa coefficients
from geometry) is desirable and is the subject of the roadmap.

\subsection{Information criteria}
We caution that AIC and BIC are derived in the asymptotic regime
\(n \to \infty\) with fixed \(k\). Here \(k/n = 11/10 > 1\), beyond the
asymptotic regime; the values are not meaningful and we do not report
them. The residuals \(<10^{-15}\) reflect the exactly solvable
structure of the quark fit, not an exceptionally good agreement
beyond the model's degrees of freedom.

\section{Geometric interpretation of the Yukawa coefficients}
\label{sec:yukawa_origin}
The numerical values of the coefficients exhibit a clear hierarchical
pattern. In a 6D setup, the three generations can be localized at the
fixed points \(z_1 = 0\), \(z_2 = (1+\tau)/3\), and
\(z_3 = 2(1+\tau)/3\). The Yukawa couplings are given by the overlap
integral:
\begin{equation}
c_i \sim A_i \, e^{- \alpha \, d^2_{jk}},
\end{equation}
where \(A_i\) are model-dependent prefactors. The distances squared in
the Kähler metric are:
\begin{align}
d_{12}^2 &= d_{23}^2 = \frac{1 + 2\mathrm{Re}\tau + |\tau|^2}{9\,\mathrm{Im}\tau}, \\
d_{13}^2 &= 4\,d_{12}^2.
\end{align}
For the fitted moduli \(\tau_u\) and \(\tau_d\), these distances
naturally produce the observed exponential hierarchies. In the
up-sector, the extreme suppression of \(a_u \sim 10^{-5}\) corresponds
to the largest distance \(d_{13}^2\), while \(b_u \sim 7\) corresponds
to the shorter adjacent fixed points and requires an \(\mathcal{O}(1)\)
prefactor \(A_i\). While this mechanism does not analytically predict
the exact values (as the spread of \(\alpha\) across different
coefficients is about a factor of 1.7), it provides a natural physical
explanation for their vast hierarchy.

\section{Flux stabilization of the moduli}
\label{sec:flux_stabilization}
The complex structure moduli are dynamically favored by internal
fluxes. Following the GVW framework, the superpotential is
\(W(\tau) = a_0 + a_1 \tau + a_2 \tau^2\), where \(a_i \in \mathbb{Z}\)
are quantized flux numbers. Moduli stabilization requires the F-term
condition:
\begin{equation}
D_\tau W = \partial_\tau W + (\partial_\tau K) W = 0.
\end{equation}
By performing a discrete search over integer flux configurations, we
find that the phenomenological values
\(\tau_u \approx -0.158 + 2.385i\) and
\(\tau_d \approx 0.369 + 1.541i\) lie near the supersymmetric points
of the following fluxes:
\begin{align}
\text{Up-sector:} \quad & (a_0, a_1, a_2) = (-17, 0, 1), \\
\text{Down-sector:} \quad & (a_0, a_1, a_2) = (-7, -1, 1).
\end{align}
At these configurations, the flux potential yields small residuals:
\(|D_\tau W| \approx 0.16\) for the up-sector and \(0.14\) for the
down-sector. While these values are not exactly zero, they indicate
that the phenomenological moduli are \emph{near-supersymmetric points}
favored by discrete topology. The fluxes are found by a discrete search;
deriving them from the topological data of the orbifold is left for
future work. Exact moduli stabilization requires a full analysis of
the scalar potential, including the Hessian and non-perturbative
corrections, which is also left for future work.

\section{Open problems}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{Lepton sector (negative result).} The \(3_S\) sector with
\(Y^{(2)}\) and \(Y^{(4)}\) cannot simultaneously reproduce the PMNS
angles and the mass-squared differences (best joint fit
\(\chi^2 \approx 39\)). Adding \(M_{3_S}(Y^{(6)})\) gives \(\chi^2 = 0\)
formally, but \(\tau_l\) becomes undetermined. A viable lepton sector
requires a different modular structure (\S8).
\item \textbf{Borderline overfitting.} \(N_{\rm dof} = -1\): the model
fits 10 observables with 11 effective parameters. A reduction of
parameters is desirable and is the subject of the roadmap.
\item \textbf{Flatness of \(\mathrm{Re}\,\tau\).} The flatness of the
real parts of the moduli remains an open problem (Appendix~E).
\item \textbf{Status of \(\alpha_G\) and \(\varepsilon\).} Both remain
postulates/ansätze (\S3, \S4).
\item \textbf{LOO instability.} The Leave-One-Out test shows extreme
instability, typical for models with \(N_{\rm dof} < 0\).
\item \textbf{Hierarchy problem.} The model explains the fermion
hierarchy but does not address the Higgs hierarchy. This is beyond
the scope of the present work.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions}
\textbf{What has been done:}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item A quark-sector fit with 10 effective parameters fitting
9 observables, plus the ALP misalignment angle \(\theta_i\) fitting
\(\Omega_a h^2\): 11 effective parameters, 10 observables,
\(N_{\rm dof} = -1\).
\item Quark sector: \(\chi^2 < 10^{-15}\) (machine limit). The
hierarchical Yukawa coefficients are qualitatively explained by
geometric fixed-point overlap with \(\alpha \sim 10\) and
\(\mathcal{O}(1)\) prefactors.
\item Moduli: the phenomenological values of \(\tau_u,\tau_d\) lie near
the supersymmetric points favored by discrete GVW fluxes.
\item ALP sector: \(f_a = 1.9801\times10^{11}\)~GeV,
\(m_a = 28.407~\mu\)eV, \(\nu_a = 6.8688\)~GHz, testable at
ADMX/HAYSTAC; black hole superradiance window at
\(M_{\rm BH} \sim 10^{25}\)~kg.
\item Lepton sector: negative result. The \(3_S\) structure with
\(Y^{(2)}\) and \(Y^{(4)}\) is insufficient; adding \(Y^{(6)}\)
destroys predictivity. A different lepton sector is required (\S8).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Verdict.} The model is a phenomenological quark-sector + ALP
construction. It reproduces the 9 quark observables with high precision,
makes one independently testable prediction (ALP dark matter at
\(6.87\)~GHz), and reports a negative result on the lepton sector.
The borderline overfitting (\(N_{\rm dof}=-1\)) is acknowledged, and
parameter reduction is left for future work.
