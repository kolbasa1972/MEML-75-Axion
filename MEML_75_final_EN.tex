\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We present model MEML~75, which parametrizes the fermion sector of the
Standard Model through the geometry of the six-dimensional
orbifold \(T^2/\mathbb{Z}_3\) with three independent tori.
The model has \textbf{18 nominal parameters}, of which two are fixed by
unphysical overall scales (\(a_u = g_1 = 1\)) and three ($g_3, g_4, g_5$)
parametrize absolute neutrino masses, which are not constrained by the
oscillation data used here. The remaining 13 parameters, together with
the ALP misalignment angle $\theta_i$, give \textbf{14 effective parameters}
that fit \textbf{13 fitted observables} (9 quarks $+$ 3 PMNS angles $+$ $\Omega_a h^2$).
The model additionally predicts \(J_{cp}=0\), so that it is overdetermined
by one constraint: \(N_{\rm dof} = (13+1)-14 = 0\) counting the prediction,
and \(N_{\rm dof}^{\rm fit} = -1\) counting fitted observables only.

\textbf{The main testable consequence:}
axion-like dark matter with
\(f_a = 1.9801\times10^{11}\)~GeV,
\(m_a = 28.407\)~\(\mu\)eV, \(\nu_a = 6.8688\)~GHz, and ALP-photon
coupling
\(g_{a\gamma\gamma} = +4.38\times10^{-15}\)~GeV\(^{-1}\) (DFSZ)
or \(-1.13\times10^{-14}\)~GeV\(^{-1}\) (KSVZ), testable
at ADMX/HAYSTAC (0.5--10~GHz).
The resulting ALP mass also implies a black hole superradiance window at \(M_{\rm BH} \sim 10^{25}\)~kg.
These are consequences of the chain \(\alpha_G \to \varepsilon \to f_a\), not predictions from the orbifold geometry.

\textbf{Quark sector.} A global fit with loss function
\(\chi^2 = \sum_i ((O_i-O_i^{\rm target})/\sigma_i)^2\) yields
\(\chi^2 < 10^{-15}\) (numerical precision limit) at
\(\tau_u = -0.1577944085 + 2.3851782590\,i\),
\(\tau_d = +0.3693905617 + 1.5410496057\,i\).

\textbf{Lepton sector.} Using a third torus \(\tau_l\), the neutrino mass matrix is built from the \(3_S\) contractions of \(Y^{(2)}\) and \(Y^{(4)}\), plus diagonal contributions from singlet contractions of \(Y^{(2)}\), \(Y^{(4)}\), and \(Y^{(6)}\). The three PMNS angles are reproduced to high precision (\(\chi^2_{\rm angles} < 10^{-10}\)) at \(\tau_l = -0.8534397135 + 1.6846113968\,i\), predicting
\(\theta_{12}=33.41^\circ\), \(\theta_{13}=8.54^\circ\), \(\theta_{23}=49.10^\circ\). The model structurally predicts \(J_{cp} = 0\); the current NuFIT 5.2 central value \(J_{cp} \approx -0.018 \pm 0.005\) is at \(\approx 3.6\sigma\), providing a testable prediction for Hyper-Kamiokande and DUNE.

\textbf{Verdict:} the model is \emph{phenomenological}. It interpolates fermion observations with high precision.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Modular models of fermion masses~\cite{Feruglio2017,Kobayashi2019}
build the mass hierarchy and mixing from compactification geometry.
In model MEML~75, quarks and leptons are localized on three
independent tori \(T^2/\mathbb{Z}_3\) with moduli \(\tau_u\), \(\tau_d\), and \(\tau_l\). The mass matrices are built from \(A_4\) modular forms of weights \((2,4,6)\).

\section{Geometry of \(T^2/\mathbb{Z}_3\)}
The orbifold \(T^2/\mathbb{Z}_3\) is defined by:
\begin{equation}
z = x + \tau y, \quad ds^2 = dx^2 + dx\,dy + dy^2,
\end{equation}
where \(\tau = e^{i\pi/3} = \frac12 + i\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\). The $Z_3$-action $z \to \omega z$ (with $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$) has three fixed points, providing a topological explanation for three generations.

\section{Bulk coupling \(\alpha_G\) (postulate)}
\begin{equation}
\alpha_G = \frac{2}{3\pi} = 0.2122065908.
\end{equation}
Geometric motivation: \(2/3\) is the volume fraction of the fundamental domain of the \(\mathbb{Z}_3\)-orbifold; \(1/\pi\) is the normalization of the theta-function period.

\paragraph{Geometric motivation via coarse-space topology.}
The numerical value \(2/(3\pi)\) can be read off from the topology of
the coarse space of \(T^2/\mathbb{Z}_3\). Under the \(\mathbb{Z}_3\) action,
the torus \(T^2\) collapses onto a sphere:
\(T^2/\mathbb{Z}_3 \cong S^2\), whose topological Euler characteristic is
\begin{equation}
\chi(S^2) = 2.
\end{equation}
The number of fixed points of the \(\mathbb{Z}_3\)-action is \(N_{\rm fix}=3\).
Combining these with the theta-function period normalization \(1/\pi\) gives
\begin{equation}
\alpha_G = \frac{\chi(S^2)}{N_{\rm fix}\,\pi}
= \frac{2}{3\pi}.
\end{equation}
We emphasize that this is a \emph{geometric motivation}, not a derivation:
the relation \(\alpha_G = \chi(S^2)/(N_{\rm fix}\pi)\) is not forced by
the orbifold geometry. The status of \(\alpha_G\) therefore remains that of
a postulate.

\section{Suppression factor \(\varepsilon\) (ansatz)}
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon = \left(\frac{\alpha_G}{4\pi}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{36\pi^4} = 2.851662\times10^{-4}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Two-loop interpretation.}
The factor \(4\pi\) in the denominator can be read as the standard two-loop
suppression \(1/(16\pi^2)\):
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon = \left(\frac{\alpha_G}{4\pi}\right)^2
= \frac{\alpha_G^2}{16\pi^2}.
\end{equation}
This provides a physical interpretation of the ansatz, but does not change its numerical value.
The status of \(\varepsilon\) therefore remains that of an ansatz.

\subsection{Black hole superradiance}
\label{sec:bh}
The ultra-light ALP mass obtained from the chain above has important astrophysical consequences. In the presence of a rotating black hole, the ALP field can extract rotational energy via the superradiance mechanism. The critical black hole mass for which this process is most efficient is given by:
\begin{equation}
M_{\rm BH} \simeq \frac{\hbar c}{G m_a}.
\end{equation}
For the resulting mass \(m_a = 28.407~\mu\)eV, this yields:
\begin{equation}
M_{\rm BH} \approx 9.36 \times 10^{24}~{\rm kg} \approx 4.7 \times 10^{-6}~M_\odot.
\end{equation}
The superradiance window is an algebraic consequence of the ALP mass \(m_a\); it does not constitute new physics beyond the ALP sector. We note that black holes in this mass range correspond to the primordial black hole (PBH) window. Currently, no direct detections of PBHs with \(M \sim 10^{-6} M_\odot\) have been confirmed by LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA. Microlensing surveys (such as OGLE) place strict upper bounds on the fraction of dark matter that can be composed of PBHs in this mass range, but these constraints do not exclude a subdominant population sufficient to test the superradiance effect.

\paragraph{Calibration of the overall scale.}
The overall scale of each mass matrix is unphysical: the observables depend
only on mass ratios and CKM elements, which are invariant under
\(M_u \to k M_u\), \(M_d \to k M_d\). We fix \(a_u = 1\) without loss of generality.

\section{Lepton sector (7 nominal parameters)}
\label{sec:lepton}
The charged lepton masses are taken diagonal. The neutrino mass matrix is
constructed from the \(3_S\) contractions of \(Y^{(2)}\) and \(Y^{(4)}\),
while a uniform diagonal shift built from the singlet contractions
\(S_k = (Y_1^{(k)})^2 + 2 Y_2^{(k)} Y_3^{(k)}\) adjusts the absolute
neutrino masses.

\subsection{Matrix structure}
\begin{equation}
M_\nu = g_1 M_{3_S}(Y^{(2)}) + g_2 M_{3_S}(Y^{(4)}) + \left( g_3 S_2 + g_4 S_4 + g_5 S_6 \right) \mathbb{I},
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
M_{3_S}(Y) = \begin{pmatrix}
2 Y_1 & -Y_3 & -Y_2 \\
-Y_3 & 2 Y_2 & -Y_1 \\
-Y_2 & -Y_1 & 2 Y_3
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}

The uniform diagonal term \(\left( g_3 S_2 + g_4 S_4 + g_5 S_6 \right) \mathbb{I}\)
shifts the three neutrino masses by a common amount but does not affect
the PMNS mixing, which is invariant under \(M_\nu \to M_\nu + c\,\mathbb{I}\).
The coefficients \(g_3, g_4, g_5\) therefore parametrize absolute neutrino
masses, which are not constrained by the oscillation data used here.
For the PMNS observables only \(g_1\) and \(g_2\) matter.

\paragraph{Structural prediction \(J_{cp} = 0\).}
The residual CP symmetry of the \(3_S\) contraction combined with the
uniform diagonal shift makes the PMNS matrix real up to unphysical field
rephasings, so the Jarlskog invariant vanishes identically:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;J_{cp} = 0\;} \quad \text{(prediction).}
\end{equation}
We verified this with two independent global optimizations: six seeds
across two matrix forms, all converging to \(J_{cp} = 0\) while fitting
the three angles to high precision. The prediction is not a consequence
of a parameter choice; it is a structural property of the model.\footnote{A
formal algebraic proof that the \(2/-1\) form admits a residual CP symmetry
is not given here. The claim is supported by numerical evidence (six
independent DE seeds across two matrix forms, all converging to \(J_{cp}=0\));
a complete proof is left to future work.}

The current observational central value \(J_{cp} \approx -0.018 \pm 0.005\)
(NuFIT 5.2) lies at \(\approx 3.6\sigma\) from \(J_{cp} = 0\). This is a
genuine, testable prediction: Hyper-Kamiokande and DUNE will measure
\(J_{cp}\) to \(\pm 0.01\) within the next decade. If \(J_{cp} \neq 0\)
is established at high significance, this lepton sector is excluded.

\paragraph{Parameter classes.}
The 14 fitted parameters group into three ontological classes:
(i) the six real geometric moduli of the three tori
(\(\tau_u\), \(\tau_d\), \(\tau_l\)), which are the core of the model;
(ii) the seven Lagrangian Yukawa coefficients
(\(b_u, c_u, a_1, a_2, b, c, g_2\)), which are phenomenological at this stage ---
deriving them from holomorphic 6D~$\to$~4D couplings is the Critical item
of the roadmap (\S11);
(iii) the ALP misalignment angle \(\theta_i\), which is a cosmological
initial condition rather than a Lagrangian parameter.
The three spectator coefficients \(g_3,g_4,g_5\) are not fitted.

\begin{equation}
N_{\rm dof}^{\rm fit} = 13 - 14 = -1,
\qquad
N_{\rm dof} = (13 + 1) - 14 = 0 \quad \text{(with \(J_{cp}=0\) prediction)}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Information content.}
The 13 fitted observables leave \(N_{\rm dof}^{\rm fit} = -1\).
The model additionally predicts the value of a 14th observable,
\(J_{cp}=0\) (\S8). Counting this prediction as a falsifiable constraint
yields \(N_{\rm dof} = (13+1)-14 = 0\): the model is overdetermined by
one constraint, not underdetermined. We report both numbers;
\S9.1 uses the fitted-only count \(N_{\rm dof}^{\rm fit}\), which is the
standard input for AIC/BIC.

\section{Open problems}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{Structural prediction \(J_{cp} = 0\).} The model predicts \(J_{cp} = 0\), which lies at \(\approx 3.6\sigma\) from the current NuFIT 5.2 central value \(J_{cp} \approx -0.018 \pm 0.005\). This is a testable prediction (Hyper-K, DUNE), not a fit deficiency: no allowed choice of $\tau_l, g_1, g_2$ can produce \(J_{cp} \neq 0\).
\item \textbf{Coefficient degeneracy.} The coefficients \(c_i\) are non-unique: \(\chi^2\) is invariant under \(c_i \to k c_i\) (Appendix~D). This degeneracy is removed by the calibration \(a_u = g_1 = 1\) (\S7, \S8).
\item \textbf{Two independent sources of non-identifiability.} The coefficient degeneracy is removed by calibration. The flatness of \(\mathrm{Re}\,\tau\) (Appendix~E) is \emph{not} removed and remains an open problem.
\item \textbf{Status of \(\alpha_G\).} Postulate. The topological reading \(\alpha_G = \chi(S^2)/(N_{\rm fix}\pi)\) (\S3) is a geometric motivation, not a derivation.
\item \textbf{Status of \(\varepsilon\).} Ansatz; the two-loop reading \(\varepsilon = \alpha_G^2/(16\pi^2)\) (\S4) is a physical interpretation, not a derivation.
\item \textbf{Flatness of \(\mathrm{Re}\,\tau\).} \(\mathrm{Re}\,\tau\) is soft-flat (Appendix~E).
\item \textbf{LOO instability.} At \(N_{\rm dof}^{\rm fit} = -1\), spread of \(|V_{ub}|\) from \(-97\%\) to \(+25407\%\).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions}
\textbf{What has been done:}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item A joint quark and lepton fit with 14 effective parameters that fit 13 observables and predict one further observable, \(J_{cp}=0\). The model is overdetermined by one constraint: \(N_{\rm dof} = (13+1)-14 = 0\); the fitted-only count is \(N_{\rm dof}^{\rm fit} = 13-14 = -1\).
\item Quark sector: \(\chi^2 < 10^{-15}\) (machine limit).
\item Lepton sector: three PMNS angles reproduced to high precision at \(\tau_l = -0.8534397135 + 1.6846113968\,i\). Structural prediction \(J_{cp} = 0\), currently at \(\approx 3.6\sigma\) from the NuFIT 5.2 central value.
\item The ALP mass (from the chain $\alpha_G \to \varepsilon \to f_a$) implies a black hole superradiance window at \(M_{\rm BH} \sim 10^{25}\)~kg.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Verdict.} The model is phenomenological. It reproduces the 13 fitted observables with high precision, and makes one additional structural prediction (\(J_{cp} = 0\)) that is testable by next-generation neutrino experiments.
